Source Type: Student newspaper article (The Hilltop)
Title: Enrollment Increase Puts Strain On Students, Teachers in College of Arts and Sciences
Source: https://thehilltoponline.com/2023/02/27/enrollment-increase-puts-strain-on-students-teachers-in-college-of-arts-and-sciences/
Date: February 27, 2023

Summary of reported information:
The College of Arts and Sciences (COAS) faced challenges accommodating a growing student body, reported at 3,973 enrolled students by Dean Rubin Patterson. The increase in enrollment reportedly pushed COAS faculty into larger class sizes than before.

Kenneth Anderson (identified elsewhere in Hilltop coverage as associate provost for undergraduate studies) noted that nationwide faculty turnover has been a factor, while Howard has simultaneously seen increased student enrollment during and after the pandemic, unlike many peer institutions.

A student named Fleming, dealing with a popular major, reported that a lack of response from faculty advisors was a major reason her course registration took so long, including being redirected to an advisor no longer employed at the university. A former biology advisor, identified as Young, suggested some students could better manage registration stress by using university-provided solutions (such as course substitutions for divisional requirements) rather than panicking when a required class is full.

Relevance to this project: Documents how rising enrollment indirectly affects the professor experience described in individual reviews - larger class sizes and advisor availability are structural conditions that shape what a review of a specific professor or course might reflect in a given semester.
